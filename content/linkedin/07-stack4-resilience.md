# Post 7: Stack 4 — Adaptive Resilience

Efficient systems can be brittle. I think about this a lot.

You optimize for current conditions. You get lean. And then conditions change — and there's no slack in the system to absorb it.

Supply chains break. Key customers leave. Regulations shift overnight. Input prices spike 40% in a quarter.

Anyone in agriculture knows this feeling. The question isn't whether disruption will happen. It's whether your operation can take the hit without breaking.

Stack 4 is about designing for volatility. And the key is that by this point you have real data from Stacks 1-3 to model with.

Four scenarios I push people to plan:

Plan A: Business as usual
Plan B: Moderate disruption — supply shock, price spike, losing a key customer
Plan C: Major shift — regulatory change, market transformation, climate event
Plan D: Opportunity — an emerging trend that could accelerate in your favor

Most businesses only have Plan A. Some have a vague Plan B. Almost nobody has modeled C and D with actual operational numbers.

What you're building: a vulnerability map, early warning systems for external shifts, response protocols, and redundancy that pays for itself in normal operations.

That last part is important. Good redundancy isn't waste. It's capacity that generates value when things are calm and saves you when they aren't.

The little guys who do this work are the ones still standing when the disruption hits. They just don't get the headlines for it.

What's the single point of failure that keeps you up at night?
