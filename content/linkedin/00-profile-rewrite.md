# LinkedIn Profile Rewrite

## Headline (max 220 characters)

Sustainability should pay for itself. I built the Five Stacks Framework to make that happen for agricultural SMEs.

## About

I spent a decade watching sustainability get sold wrong.

Big companies hire sustainability officers, publish reports nobody reads, and call it progress. Meanwhile small agricultural operators — the ones actually doing the work — can't prove what they're doing because they don't have the budget for the report or the consultant.

Framing sustainability as a moral imperative doesn't work. People do things because they see a benefit.

So I built a system around that idea.

The Five Stacks Framework is a sequential approach where each step pays for the next:

1. Core Metrics — see your operation clearly, build an audit-ready baseline
2. Operational Efficiency — find and fix the margin leaks (15-30% cost reduction is common)
3. Circular Value Creation — monetize by-products, reduce disposal costs, close loops
4. Adaptive Resilience — lower input dependency, build capacity to absorb shocks
5. Regenerative Advantage — earn certifications and premium positioning backed by proof, not claims

The sequence matters. You can't skip to certifications without the data underneath. But each stack funds the next — measurement costs time, efficiency saves money, circularity generates money, resilience protects money, positioning multiplies money.

I built this for operators who can't afford to throw money at sustainability and hope it works. Who need every step to justify itself.

Background: MS in Natural Resources Management (Humboldt University, Berlin). 10 years building Ecosystems United.

## Connection Strategy (Starting From Zero, No Personal Network)

### Phase 1: Before you post (this week)

1. FOLLOW first, don't connect yet. Follow 50-100 people in these categories:
   - Sustainability consultants (especially ones posting about CSRD, SMEs)
   - Agricultural industry commentators
   - Supply chain / procurement people who talk about Scope 3
   - Ecolabel and certification organizations
   - Other people building sustainability tools or frameworks

   Search terms: "CSRD SME", "sustainability agriculture", "Scope 3 supply chain",
   "ecolabels", "circular economy agriculture", "sustainability consultant"

2. COMMENT on their posts. This is the #1 growth lever on LinkedIn.
   - Not "Great post!" — that's invisible
   - Add something. Disagree slightly. Share a related observation. Ask a genuine question.
   - 3-5 thoughtful comments per day, every day
   - This gets your name and headline in front of their audience before you've posted anything

3. Join groups (low priority but helps with search visibility):
   - CSRD / ESG reporting groups
   - Sustainable agriculture groups
   - SME / small business groups (European ones especially)

### Phase 2: First week of posting (Posts 1-2)

4. After each post, send connection requests to:
   - People who liked or commented on posts you commented on (you already have visibility with them)
   - Authors of posts you've been commenting on
   - ALWAYS include a note: "I've been following your work on [specific topic]. Building something similar for agricultural SMEs — would be good to connect."
   - Never send blank connection requests. Always a note.
   - 10-15 requests per day max (LinkedIn flags mass-connecting)

5. When someone comments on YOUR post:
   - Reply to every comment within 2 hours (algorithm boost)
   - Then visit their profile and connect if relevant

### Phase 3: Ongoing (Weeks 2-5, during the series)

6. Keep commenting on other people's posts — this matters more than your own posting, especially early on
7. Engage with anyone who engages with you — this builds a small but real community
8. Share/repost relevant posts from people you follow with a short take of your own added

### What NOT to do
- Don't use LinkedIn automation tools (they get accounts flagged)
- Don't connect with randoms just to inflate numbers — 50 engaged connections > 500 dead ones
- Don't post and disappear — the commenting is more important than the posting for the first month
- Don't buy followers or engagement pods

### Realistic timeline
- Week 1-2: 20-50 connections, mostly from commenting
- Week 4: 50-100 connections, some organic from posts
- Month 2-3: 100-300 if you're consistent with commenting + posting
- Your first viral-ish post (1000+ impressions) will probably come in month 2-3, not week 1

The algorithm rewards consistency over quality spikes. Showing up 5 days a week with comments + 2 posts beats one brilliant post followed by silence.
