# Post 5: Stack 2 — Operational Efficiency

Every operation leaks value. Every single one.

Energy heating empty space. Materials becoming waste. Processes with steps nobody questions anymore. Labor on things that don't move the needle.

Most operators know they have inefficiencies. They just don't know where, how much they cost, or which ones to fix first.

That's why Stack 2 comes after Stack 1. Without the baseline, efficiency work is guesswork. With it, it's surgery.

Four types of leaks I look for:

Energy — equipment running when it shouldn't
Material — waste, spoilage, overuse, rework
Process — redundant steps, bottlenecks, handoff failures
Information — decisions made without data, knowledge that walks out the door

For each one: size it (annual cost), assess the effort, calculate the payback.

Here's the thing people miss — not every leak is worth fixing. Some cost more to plug than they drain. Stack 2 is about prioritization, not perfection.

You know it's working when quick wins are closed, margin improvement is quantified (not just assumed), and your operation is tight enough that the next stack — circularity — won't just leak out faster than you capture it.

The margin you recover here funds everything that comes next. That's the design. Each stack pays for the next one.
