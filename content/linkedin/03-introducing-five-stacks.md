# Post 3: Introducing Five Stacks

Most sustainability frameworks tell you what to report. They don't tell you how to actually build the capability that makes reporting possible — or useful.

That gap is what frustrated me enough to build something.

The Five Stacks Framework is a sequential system. Each stack creates the conditions for the next:

Stack 1: Core Metrics — see your operation clearly
Stack 2: Efficiency — stop the leaks
Stack 3: Circularity — capture value you're losing
Stack 4: Resilience — absorb shocks without breaking
Stack 5: Regenerative — compound your advantage

The key word is sequential. You can't skip steps — and that's a feature, not a bug.

You can't position for premium markets (Stack 5) without proof from Stacks 1-4. You can't build resilience (Stack 4) without baseline data. You can't fix leaks (Stack 2) if you can't see them (Stack 1).

It's systematic. It's not glamorous. But every improvement does double duty — operational improvement AND regulatory requirement. Margin improvement AND emissions reduction.

I built this for the small operators who don't have a sustainability department. Who need the work to pay for itself as they go. Who are tired of frameworks that assume you have a budget and a team.
