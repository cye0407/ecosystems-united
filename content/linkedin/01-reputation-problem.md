# Post 1: The Reputation Problem

Sustainability has a reputation problem. And honestly, it earned it.

I've watched companies hire sustainability officers, publish glossy reports, and carry on exactly as before. "Green" became a marketing term divorced from anything operational.

And the small operators — the ones actually doing the work? They don't get the credit. They don't have the budget for the report. They just quietly run tighter ships than most of the big guys making press releases.

Here's what I keep coming back to:

Framing sustainability as a moral imperative doesn't work. It just doesn't. People don't change their operations because someone told them they should care more.

People do things because they see a benefit.

And the thing is — the benefit is right there. The operational improvements behind real sustainability are the same ones that make a business better. Lower input costs. Less waste. Captured value. Resilience when things go sideways.

The problem isn't sustainability itself. The problem is we've been selling it wrong.

What's been your experience — burden or business case?
