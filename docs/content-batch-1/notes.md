## Note 1 — the shape of the cost

Cover crops aren't an investment, they're an operating cost — roughly €55/ha for seed and establishment, every single year you keep the cover in. That reframe sounds pedantic until you meet the farmer who budgeted it once and then got the same bill three autumns running. Practices get abandoned over wrong shapes, not wrong sizes.

## Note 2 — rank returns by defensibility

The cover-crop pitch usually leads with soil health and climate resilience and mentions fertiliser savings in passing. The defensible maths runs exactly backwards: fertiliser first (hairy vetch can fix 90–160 lbs N/acre — that's an invoice you can point at), yield second (~4.5 bu/acre in corn studies, but weather eats small effects), and the poetic stuff last. If someone's selling you a practice, check which order they lead with.

## Note 3 — I made my own calculator dip

The first coefficient I hard-coded into my regenerative ROI model is a loss: minus 8% of gross margin in year one. A tool that overpromises gets used once, disappoints with a two-year lag, and poisons trust in the whole category. A tool that undersells and gets beaten by reality creates a farmer who tells the neighbours.

## Note 4 — the year-two exodus is rational

USDA-SARE data puts average cover-crop break-even at about three years. So the farmer who quits in year two isn't short-sighted — they're responding sensibly to a curve nobody showed them. They were promised "pays for itself" and the first eighteen months quietly cost money. The failure is upstream, in the pitch.

## Note 5 — if it's really that good, it survives conservative modelling

Steady-state studies claim regenerative systems run 70–120% more profitable than conventional; one four-farm case series averaged 176% ROI. Maybe. My test is simpler: does the transition still pencil out with an 8% year-one yield dip, input savings that take five years to mature, and zero assumed subsidies? For a lot of operations it does — slowly, dipping first, the way real things do.

## Note 6 — the subsidy line I refuse to auto-fill

European eco-scheme payments for cover crops commonly land somewhere between €50 and €130/ha — a range wide enough to flip a marginal transition either way. Which is exactly why my calculator won't assume one. Rates vary that much by country and scheme, so you enter your own or you see the economics bare. If a plan only works with a subsidy someone else pre-filled, you don't have a plan yet.
